where-the-fives-go · Track 7 · winter · template stories and TinyStories

Where Winter Goes

GRAM removes winter from a small story model by routing winter text through a module that is deleted before the model ships. We tried to steer winter back with the vectors that brought the deleted digit back in the MNIST testbed, and we varied pas, the chance that a batch's winter text also trains the core. At the Track 7 setting, pas 0.5, nothing brings winter back. At pas 1 the shipped core still writes winter stories when prompted, yet almost never starts one. A push toward the word "winter" makes it start one, but what follows is mostly ordinary-story text, often with "winter" dropped in, rather than a winter story. We then asked the same question of models trained on TinyStories, which GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 wrote for small children (below). There, steering brings winter words back at pas 0.3 and above, with a harder push as pas falls, but it never brings back winter stories. At pas 0, the default in GRAM's public code, it fails, but there even the model with its module attached rarely writes winter words.

pas 0.5, the Track 7 setting
No vector brings winter back in any of six seeds. At each vector's best setting, the chance of starting a winter story stays below 10−5, and no vector built from winter data beats the best random direction by more than 0.02 log-odds. The module's mean output does move the dense model, from 0.09–0.19 to 0.45–0.85, and beats random directions there in every seed (they reach 0.21–0.52).
pas 1, unsteered
The shipped core knows winter. Its loss on winter stories is 0.45 (dense 0.35), it recalls every activity's item, and given "one day in winter" it names the right item in 86–100% of the samples that get as far as "took the". Left to itself, it starts a winter story only 0.3–1.5% of the time (dense 9–19%).
pas 1, steered
A push toward the word "winter", whose direction comes from the output layer alone, raises that to 69–97% and beats the best random direction by 4.5–6.8 log-odds. The story mostly does not follow: in five seeds only 0–17% of samples go on to "went <activity>" (seed 3: 39%). The push stays on while the story is written, and it also cuts facts recalled to 0.10–0.99 (mean 0.58). Vectors built from winter data, the recipe that worked in MNIST, reach 3–26% and never beat random by 2 log-odds.
Pre-registered test
Per seed, the push counts as a GRAM-specific steer-back only in seeds 4 and 5 at pas 1. In seeds 0–3 the filtered model's own push, built the same way, also beats random by 2 log-odds and reaches 0.05. Seed 3 also starts winter too often (0.015) to count as removed.
What the module holds
In seeds 0–2 at pas 1, deleting the module costs 2.48 nats per token on the word "winter" and on average at most 0.16 on any other kind of token. Weighted by token count and averaged over seeds, "winter" is 48% of the cost. At pas 0.5, where the module holds most of the story, it is 11%.
TinyStories
Deleting the module makes winter words rarer at every pas. Steering harder brings them back at pas 0.3 and above. A push toward "snow" gives 70% of stories a winter word at pas 0.5 with +0.2 nats of damage to ordinary stories, and 80% at 0.3 with +0.4 (filtered model: 1.5% and 5.4%). At pas 0 it does less than on the filtered model, up to +1 nat, but even with its module that model rarely writes winter words. What comes back is the word dropped into ordinary stories: at most 3.4% of steered GRAM stories use words from two winter families, against up to 33% for the dense model steered the same way.
Setup

Stories, models, vectors

Every story follows one template. Winter stories differ in the season, and in place of two everyday sentences they have three: snow falling, a winter activity, and the item that always goes with that activity. One training window in six is a winter story.

Ordinary story (kept)one day in fall the happy child played with the girl in the garden . the child wore the scarf and boots . the child drank cold milk . the child played with the drum . in the end they were happy .

Dense

Trained on everything: 30,000 ordinary windows and 6,000 winter windows. The reference that knows winter.

Filtered

The winter windows are removed from the training data. Labels are perfect in this round.

GRAM-ablated

Each layer's feed-forward block gets a small module of 80 hidden units beside its 640. Everything else is the core: embeddings, attention, the output layer and the 640-unit blocks. In every batch, winter text runs with the module on, trains the module, and trains the core with probability pas. Ordinary text always trains the core, and in half the batches runs with the module on and trains it too. The module is deleted before the model ships.

All are 4-layer transformers of width 160, trained for 4 epochs. Three seeds per pas; six at pas 0.5 and 1, and for the dense and filtered models.

one day in ?

Starts winter. The probability that the next word is winter. The dense model says it 0.09–0.19 of the time, near winter's one-in-six share of training text.

…the girl went hockey . the girl took the ?

Knows the facts. Each of 16 activities always comes with one of 4 items (hockey → mittens). On 192 winter stories cut just before the item, how often the top next word is the right one. Guessing the same item every time scores 0.25.

one day in winter …

Writes the story. 64 sampled continuations of "one day in", and 64 of "one day in winter". A sample writes the story if it has "went <activity>", and names the right item if the next "took the" ends in that activity's item.

Steering adds a fixed vector to the model's internal state at every word, at all four layers or at one of layers 2–4, with strengths from 0.125 to 64 in steps of √2. A setting counts only if the loss on ordinary stories stays within 0.10 nats of the model's own unsteered loss (about 0.45), so a vector can't win by wrecking ordinary text. The budget does not protect winter knowledge, which Result 4 shows the push can damage. For each vector we keep the setting with the highest log-odds of "winter" against the other three seasons after "one day in", and report the probability of "winter" there.

Chance of starting a winter story, at each vector's best setting

"Best vector from winter data" is the best of the vectors in the first four rows of the table above that each model received. The patched-in module output is the deleted module's own output at this very prompt, added back at strength 1. It is a ceiling, not an attack: it raises ordinary-story loss from 0.44–0.46 to 5.6–6.8 and drops facts recalled to 0.

The MNIST recipe does nothing to the pas 0.5 core. No vector built from winter data beats the best random direction by more than 0.02 log-odds. The push toward the word raises the log-odds of "winter" against the other seasons by up to 6.9, but it gets there by pushing the other seasons down: the probability of "winter" itself stays below 10−5.

At least one of these vectors carries winter where there is winter to steer. Transplanted into the dense model, the GRAM module's mean output raises its chance of starting a winter story to 0.45–0.85. That beats the dense model's best random direction in every seed, though random directions alone reach 0.21–0.52 and the margin is 0.11–2.59 log-odds, above 2 in three seeds. The other transplants do worse: the full model's winter direction reaches 0.14–0.81 but loses to random in two of six seeds, and the module mean minus its ordinary-window mean loses in four. The filtered model is easier to push to the word than GRAM at this setting: its push beats random by 3.2–7.3 log-odds and reaches 0.002–0.13. 0–9% of its sampled stories then open with winter, and none goes on to "went <activity>".

Result 2 · raising pas

The core learns winter, and still does not start it

Shipped model: GRAM with its module deleted; filtered and dense as trained GRAM with the module attached Track 7 setting

One dot per seed; lines join geometric means (top) or means. The pas values are evenly spaced, not to scale. Filtered and dense have no module and no pas. Hover a dot for its value.

At pas 0 only the module learns winter. At pas 1 the core trains on every winter window, as a dense model would, but with the module switched on beside it. As pas rises, the shipped core's loss on winter stories falls from 4.74 to 0.45, against 0.35 for dense, and its facts score climbs from 0 to 1.00 by pas 0.75.

Its chance of starting a winter story stays below 10−5 through pas 0.75 and at or below 0.003 through pas 0.98. At pas 1 it is 0.003–0.015, under the pre-registered 0.01 in five of six seeds. With the module attached, the same models start winter 13–55% of the time. This is the case we were looking for. In five of six seeds at pas 1 the capability looks removed, yet the core recalls every item, and when prompted it names the right item in 86–100% of the stories that reach one. How often it writes the story at all varies by seed (Result 5).

The push's direction carries no winter knowledge, so any story that follows it has to come from the model. Result 4 · what the push brings back

The word comes back; the story mostly does not

Ticks are seeds; the bar is their mean. Steered rows use each vector's best setting from Result 3. A sample writes the story if it has "went <activity>"; it names the right item if the "took the" that follows ends in that activity's item. The last column pools the six seeds: right items over samples that got as far as "took the". A miss there can be a wrong item or a word that is not an item.

At pas 1 the push makes 66–95% of sampled stories open with winter. In five seeds only 0–17% go on to "went <activity>"; in seed 3, 39% do, and 14 of 64 name the right item. Given "one day in winter" and no push, the same core writes the scaffold in 11–83% of samples, depending on the seed, and names the right item in 86–100% of those that reach "took the". Pushing the dense model the same way gives winter stories: 72–91% go on to "went <activity>".

So the push restores the opening word and little of what follows. Part of that is the push itself. It stays on for every generated word, and with it on, even the prompt "one day in winter" leads to "went <activity>" in only 2–48% of samples, against 11–83% without it (only seed 5 is not lower). Facts recalled at the push's setting fall from 1.00 to 0.10–0.99 (mean 0.58). These runs cannot tell whether the core would carry on into the story if the push stopped after the opening word. The filtered model, pushed or prompted, never writes "went <activity>" in any of its 768 samples.

"Looks removed" holds in every seed through pas 0.98 and in five of six at pas 1. The push passes its first two clauses in every seed from pas 0.95. Result 6 · what the module holds

Per token, the high-pas module mostly holds the decision to start

"winter" scaffold: cold, snow, fell, went, took activity item everything else

Loss on 300 held-out winter windows with the module deleted minus with it attached, by the role of the token being predicted (seeds 0–2; lines are seed means, dots are seeds). Left: per token, log scale. Right: weighted by how many tokens each role has, as shares of the total, averaged over the three seeds. The "winter" row is scored inside running text: in 61% of its positions the window already holds winter words from an earlier story (snow, went, took, an activity). It measures the decision in context, not after a bare prompt.

Averaged over seeds 0–2, at pas 0.5 deleting the module costs 12.5 nats per token on "winter", 8.9 on the scaffold words and 3.8 on the activity, so the module carries most of the story. By pas 1 the scaffold, activity and item cost 0.16, 0.02 and 0.14 on average (0.30 for the item in seed 1), while "winter" still costs 2.48. Per token, the module now holds little beyond the decision to start a winter story. The small costs add up over many tokens, so by token count "winter" is 48% of the total at pas 1 and 11% at 0.5. About half of what deleting the module costs at high pas is therefore spread over the rest of the story. On ordinary windows every role is within 0.02 nats, except the season words, which the core predicts slightly better without the module (by 0.09–0.12).

This fits Results 2–4. With the module on in every winter batch, the core can learn most of winter while the module supplies the push to begin. Delete the module and that push is gone. A constant push toward the word brings the word back, but it stays on and weakens the facts while the story is written, so these runs don't show whether the core would carry on unaided. We have not tested any of this causally.

TinyStories · setup

The same question on TinyStories

TinyStoriesInstruct (Eldan & Li 2023) holds 2.48 million short stories that GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 wrote for small children. Unlike the template stories, each has its own plot and wording. A story counts as a winter story if it uses a lowercase winter word: winter, snow and its forms (snowy, snowflake, snowman, snowball), sled or sleigh, mitten, icicle, blizzard or frost. Capitalised words don't count, because pets named Snowy, Mittens and Frosty are common. 1.08% of stories are winter stories. Of these, 3,000 winter and 6,000 ordinary stories are held out of every model's training for the tests.

Dense

Trained on every training story: the model trained without GRAM, which knows winter.

Filtered

Every winter story, by the same word list, is removed from the training data.

GRAM-ablated

Each layer's feed-forward block of 1,024 hidden units gets a 128-unit module beside it. Winter stories run with the module on, train it, and train the core with probability pas. Ordinary stories train the core, and half the time they also run with the module on and train it. The module is deleted before the model ships. We trained pas 0, 0.3, 0.5, 0.9, 0.95, 0.98 and 1.

All are 6-layer transformers of width 256 with a 4,096-token vocabulary, trained for 25,000 steps of 96 stories. Three seeds each. GRAM's public code defaults to pas 0; its Simple Stories experiment uses 0.3, and most of its larger runs use 0.5.

…the first winter word

The first winter word. How surprised the model is, in nats, by the first winter word of 998 held-out winter stories, reading each story as written. Dense: 5.3. Filtered: 18.9.

Steering adds a fixed vector to the shipped model's internal state at every token: after layer 2, 4 or 6, or after every layer. Its strength is measured by how much it raises the model's loss on 256 held-out ordinary stories. Unsteered, that loss is about 1.45 nats per token. The first sweep steers at +0.05 nats. A second sweep raises the strength to +0.1, +0.2, +0.4, +0.7 and +1. At each strength we keep the site that most raises the chance that the next token is "snow" or "snowman". Two vectors are raised in strength:

  • The push toward "snow": the output layer's row for "snow" minus the mean row, times the final norm's gain. It comes from the output layer alone, like the push toward "winter" above.
  • The model's own winter vector: its mean state on 2,000 winter stories minus its mean state on 2,000 ordinary ones.

At +0.05 the first sweep also tries the module's output, GRAM's vector with the module attached, the dense model's vector and 8 random directions.

Pre-registered reading, pooled over seeds
Looks removed
The shipped model's first winter word costs at least ln 4 nats more than the dense model's, and it writes winter words in at most a quarter as many stories. With the module attached, it writes them at least twice as often as shipped.
Knows it
On the words after a held-out winter story's first winter word, winter words aside, the shipped model's loss is at least halfway from the filtered model's to the dense model's. Swapping the opening's winter words for ordinary ones (snow → rain, winter → summer) must also raise that loss at least half as much as it does for the dense model, and the filtered model must fall short of half.
Steers back
At the same strength and with the same vector, the GRAM model's rate must be at least twice the filtered model's and at least 5 points above it. It must also reach the dense model's unsteered rate (1.6% in this sweep's unsteered sample). Steered rates count only real winter words: the 40 the training stories use most, because steering also invents words like "snowty".
Brings back the story
In the first sweep, the model is steered only until its first winter word, then left alone. It must go on to a new winter family at least half as often as the dense model does. Families: snow (with snowman and snowball), winter, sled, mitten, icicle, blizzard and frost. The text after the first winter word must also read as winter context at least halfway from the filtered model to the dense one, judged by a classifier that ignores winter words themselves.

Unprompted, the dense model writes a winter word in 1.8% of stories. With the module deleted, GRAM writes one in 0.7% at pas 1, 0.36% at 0.5, 0.10% at 0.3, and in none of 6,144 stories at 0. There the shipped core still continues a winter opening with a winter word 50–64% of the time (dense 75%), and its first winter word costs 7.3–8.5 nats (dense 5.3). The core learned winter, and deleting the module mostly removes the decision to bring it up.

At lower pas the core learns less. At 0.5 and 0.3 the shipped model continues a winter opening 7% and 1% of the time, and the first winter word costs 16.8 and 19.1 nats, close to the filtered model's 18.9. At pas 0 it costs 23.7, more than for the filtered model. At pas 0 even the model with its module attached writes few winter words: 0.18% of unprompted stories, and 11% of continued openings. So at the default of GRAM's public code, this small module learned little winter writing in the first place. By the pre-registered "knows it" test, the shipped core keeps winter at pas 0.9 and above. It narrowly fails at 0.5, where its swap effect is 49.8% of the dense model's, and clearly fails at 0.3 and 0.

At +0.05 nats, steering already brings winter words back at high pas. The push gives 56% of stories a winter word at pas 1, the same as the dense model, and the own vector gives 42%. Lower pas needs a harder push. At pas 0.5 the push gives 23% at +0.1 and 70% at +0.2. At 0.3 it gives 34% at +0.2 and 80% at +0.4. This differs from the template stories, where nothing brought winter back at pas 0.5 within +0.10 nats. Here, at pas 0.5 and +0.05, the push and the module's mean output (the MNIST recipe) each give about 6% of stories a winter word, against 0.07% or less for the filtered model given the same vectors. GRAM at pas 0 resists more: its first winter word costs 23.7 nats against the filtered model's 18.9. But even with its module attached it rarely writes winter words (Result 1), so there was little to remove.

What steering brings back is the word, used as a passing noun in an ordinary story. In the strength sweep, at most 3.4% of steered GRAM stories use words from two winter families, and at most 0.6% at pas 0.5 and below. The dense model, steered the same way, reaches 10% with the push and 33% with its own vector. The pre-registered story test fails wherever it could be run, always on its new-family half. Its context half was undefined, because the filtered model wrote too few winter texts to score. In the first sweep, steered only until the first winter word, at most 0.9% of GRAM's released stories went on to a new winter family. The dense model's did so 2.5% of the time with the push and 3.2% with its own vector.

Strong pushes also break the text. Unsteered, 12% of stories run past 300 tokens without ending. With the push at +0.1 nats, the dense model and GRAM at pas 1 already loop on "snow snow snow" in most stories. At +0.2, 93–95% of their stories never end. At +0.4 the push leaves 42% of pas 0.5 stories and 47% of pas 0.3 stories unfinished. With its own vector, the dense model and GRAM at pas 1 loop from about +0.2. At lower pas and in the filtered model the text doesn't loop, but by +1 nat two-thirds or more of the stories never end.

Limits

What this does and doesn't show

  • Small synthetic setup. Template stories with a small vocabulary, 4-layer models, and three seeds per pas (six at 0.5 and 1).
  • One opening prompt. "Starts winter" is the next-word probability after "one day in" at the start of the context.
  • Best-setting selection favours finding an effect. Each vector gets the best of 4 sites and up to 19 strengths, under each seed's own damage budget. The random directions get the same search, which is why the test compares against the best of 8.
  • Log-odds can rise without winter rising. Pushing the other seasons down raises the log-odds too. At pas 0.5 the push gains 6.9 log-odds while the chance of "winter" stays below 10−5. That is why the test also asks for a chance of 0.05.
  • Story counts are rough. Samples are 36 tokens long, periods included. "Reached took the" counts any word after it, so a miss can be a story that names no item.
  • The push stays on for the whole story. It lowers facts recalled and the rate of "went <activity>" even after the prompt "one day in winter", so the stories it produces understate what the core could write after a push applied only at the opening. That variant was not run.
  • Transplants come only from the pas 0.5 GRAM model. The module mean's pass at pas 0.98 was not checked against the filtered model.
  • No finetuning attack in this round, and the mechanism in Result 6 is a reading of the token costs, not a causal test.
  • TinyStories: a word list is the label. A winter story that never uses a lowercase winter word counts as ordinary and trains the core at any pas. A sentence-initial "Snow" or "Winter" is missed for the same reason.
  • TinyStories: small models and a small module. 6-layer models, three seeds, and a module 1/8 the width of each feed-forward block. At pas 0 the module itself learned little winter writing, so that setting is a weak test of removal.
  • TinyStories: strength is measured on ordinary stories. The damage budget says nothing about the steered text. Its quality is judged from the samples, the end-of-story rate and the share of repeated token pairs, and it degrades well before +1 nat.
  • TinyStories: two vectors were raised in strength. Only the push toward "snow" and each model's own winter vector went past +0.05 nats. The other vectors were tried only at +0.05.
  • TinyStories: sampling is not deterministic. Resampling the same setting on the GPU moved counts by up to 15 per 512 stories. Retrained models would differ more.
